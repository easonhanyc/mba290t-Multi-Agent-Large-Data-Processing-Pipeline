Scope: the declared analysis scope of 200,013 reviews (analysis_200k.csv, a deterministic uniform sample of the 660,622-review corpus plus every empty-text row)

Numbers are written as value (token ID): C## IDs are the issue-level claims in `claims.csv`; every ID (C##, A## area roll-up, S## scope figure, T## trend/robustness) is listed with its value, what it measures and its source file in `memo_tokens.csv`.

## Recommendation

**billing/support.** Billing/support should get next quarter's effort, led by the Premium-only restrictions issue with severity sum 46246 (C06) across 15763 (C05) complaint and cancellation reviews, mean severity 2.933832 (C07). This is free-tier packaging and entitlement policy work - deciding what the free tier includes (skip limits, forced shuffle, repeat or rewind, song selection) and how changes are rolled out and explained - not a payment or checkout defect.

> **Check:** the baseline's highest-priority decision area is **usability** (severity sum 52983 (A05)), not the recommended area. A reviewer must confirm the memo justifies the difference.

## Next-quarter actions

1. Test clearer in-app messaging and a gentler rollout for the Premium-only restrictions issue (skip limits, forced shuffle, repeat or rewind) to see whether its complaint count below 15763 (C05) and severity sum below 46246 (C06) move down next period.
2. Review the free-tier feature boundary itself (song selection, repeat, rewind) referenced in the Premium-only restrictions issue, aiming to reduce its share of complaints below 19.7% (T22) in the next sampling window.
3. Investigate the ad-frequency experience behind the Ad interruptions issue (severity sum 23235 (C10) across 11233 (C09) reviews) as a secondary usability track, watching whether its severity sum moves down.
4. Audit the Shuffle, repeat and queue malfunctions issue (severity sum 16888 (C18) across 5685 (C17) reviews) for playlist-order bugs, tracking whether its complaint count falls below 5685 (C17) next period.

## Why

- Billing/support carries severity sum 52551 (A08) across its complaint and cancellation reviews, close behind usability's severity sum 52983 (A05) but ahead of playback/performance's severity sum 48060 (A11), with the Premium-only issue alone contributing severity sum 46246 (C06) on 15763 (C05) reviews.
- Between 2022 Jun-Oct (T01) and 2023 Jun-Oct (T02), billing/support shows the largest increase among the four decision areas, moving from share of reviews 3.8% (T10) to 13.9% (T11), a change of +10.2 pp (T12), while playback/performance fell by -4.8 pp (T15) and usability fell by -1.1 pp (T18).
- Billing/support's share of complaints 19.7% (T22) (interval 19.5% (T23) to 20.0% (T24)) does not overlap with usability's share 24.3% (T19) (interval 24.0% (T20) to 24.5% (T21)), so usability leads billing/support on share of complaints even where billing/support leads on severity sum; billing/support's interval also does not overlap playback/performance's share 16.1% (T25) (interval 15.8% (T26) to 16.3% (T27)).
- The spike month 2023-10 (T35) shows the Premium-only issue with the largest increase among that month's issues, a lift of +23.3 pp (T38) in its share of reviews.
- Robustness is mixed: the verifier's labels on sample size 2000 (T41) put playback/performance ahead of billing/support on severity sum (758 (T46) versus 660 (T48)), a labelling disagreement with the enricher's ordering that puts billing/support first (705 (T42) versus 617 (T44)), making this call provisional.

## What would change this call

- A human audit of the premium-only records without a payment word (severity sum 7283 (T57) across 2456 (T56) records) finding that most are not genuine free-tier restriction complaints would weaken the billing/support upper bound of severity sum 45268 (T58).
- The verifier's severity ordering holding for playback/performance over billing/support on a larger verified sample beyond 2000 (T41) reviews would support switching the recommendation to playback/performance.
- Billing/support's trend share reversing back toward 3.8% (T10) in the same months of the following year, rather than holding near 13.9% (T11), would undercut the trend argument for prioritizing billing/support.

## Alternatives considered

- **usability**: Usability has the highest severity sum among the four decision areas at 52983 (A05) and the highest share of complaints at 24.3% (T19), driven by Ad interruptions (severity sum 23235 (C10) across 11233 (C09) reviews) and Shuffle/repeat/queue malfunctions (severity sum 16888 (C18) across 5685 (C17) reviews). Its trend share fell by -1.1 pp (T18) between 2022 Jun-Oct (T01) and 2023 Jun-Oct (T02), and the verifier's labels rank it below playback/performance on severity sum (747 (T47) versus 758 (T46)), so it is not chosen as the lead despite its severity-sum size.
- **playback/performance**: Playback/performance's severity sum 48060 (A11) trails both usability's 52983 (A05) and billing/support's 52551 (A08), and its trend share fell by -4.8 pp (T15) between 2022 Jun-Oct (T01) and 2023 Jun-Oct (T02), moving from 9.9% (T13) to 5.1% (T14). Its lead issue, Playback stops or skips, carries severity sum 19564 (C14) across 6110 (C13) reviews. It is the verifier's top area by severity sum (758 (T46)), which argues for monitoring it but not for leading over billing/support's baseline priority and trend.
- **access/onboarding**: Access/onboarding has the lowest severity sum among the four decision areas at 11300 (A17) and the lowest share of complaints at 3.2% (T28). Its lead issue, Login and verification failures, carries severity sum 9501 (C26) across 2413 (C25) reviews. Its trend share rose only +0.8 pp (T09) between 2022 Jun-Oct (T01) and 2023 Jun-Oct (T02), the smallest movement among the four decision areas.

## Limitations

- The verifier's labels disagree with the enricher's on which area leads severity sum (playback/performance under verifier labels versus billing/support under enricher labels), so the ranking between billing/support and the top alternatives is not settled by this evidence alone.
- The period comparison spans only 2022 Jun-Oct (T01) to 2023 Jun-Oct (T02) and cannot show whether billing/support's increase will continue, reverse, or plateau beyond that window.
- The spike month 2023-10 (T35) figures describe one month's pattern in self-selected reviews and cannot establish a repeating seasonal cause for the Premium-only increase.
- Sampling intervals and verifier agreement rates describe the labeled sample, not a customer population, so they cannot quantify how many actual free-tier customers are affected.
- Unallocated complaints (computed by code from `area_rollup.json`): 29.7% (A19) of complaint and cancellation records (27137 (A01)) are general/unspecified: generic criticism (for example "worst update") that names no specific area, so they are not allocated to any of the four decision areas. If many of them refer to one area's problem, for example the same free-tier change, that area's total is understated; the labels cannot show which area that would be.
- Premium-only boundary, upper-bound sensitivity (computed by code; `analysis/premium_only_sensitivity.json`): 2456 (T56) of the 15763 (C05) `billing.premium_only` complaint and cancellation records contain no Premium or payment word (premium, subscri-, pay/paid/paying, free, buy, money, trial, $; case-insensitive), with a severity sum of 7283 (T57). If none of them belonged to billing/support, its severity sum would fall from 52551 (A08) to 45268 (T58), below usability 52983 (A05) and playback/performance 48060 (A11). This is an upper bound, not a relabelling: many of these reviews plainly describe free-tier limits without naming Premium (skip limits, forced shuffle), and they were not moved to any other area.
- Counts describe the labeled reviews in the declared analysis scope of 200,013 reviews (analysis_200k.csv, a deterministic uniform sample of the 660,622-review corpus plus every empty-text row): self-selected public app reviews, not a customer population. They carry no revenue, plan tier or confirmed cancellations, so revenue at risk cannot be computed.
- Cancellation language is expressed intent, not observed churn; nothing here estimates a causal effect on retention.
- Star ratings and helpful votes were not used to classify; severity is judged from the text under the shared rubric.
- 17828 completed records are flagged needs_review, 13 are quarantined and 0 are unfinished.
- The analysis covers a uniform random sample of the corpus, not every review; shares carry sampling error (intervals shown) and the 460,609 out-of-scope reviews were ingested but not classified.
