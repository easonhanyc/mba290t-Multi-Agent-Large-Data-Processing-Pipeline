## Recommendation

**usability.** Usability carries the highest baseline severity sum of the four decision areas, severity sum 52983 (A05) across its complaint and cancellation reviews, driven chiefly by ad interruptions (severity sum 23235 (C10) across 11233 (C09) reviews, mean severity 2.068459 (C11)), shuffle/repeat/queue malfunctions (severity sum 16888 (C18) across 5685 (C17) reviews, mean severity 2.970624 (C19)), and navigation/layout complaints after redesigns (severity sum 7795 (C30) across 3168 (C29) reviews, mean severity 2.460543 (C31)). This is core listening-experience and interface work: reducing ad frequency/placement, fixing shuffle and repeat logic, and simplifying navigation, not a payment or billing defect.

## Next-quarter actions

1. Test reduced ad frequency or improved ad placement to address ad interruptions (severity sum 23235 (C10) across 11233 (C09) reviews); success would show a drop in this issue's complaint count and severity sum.
2. Audit and fix shuffle/repeat/queue logic to address library malfunctions (severity sum 16888 (C18) across 5685 (C17) reviews); success would show a drop in this issue's complaint count and severity sum.
3. Simplify navigation and clarify redesign changes to address layout complaints (severity sum 7795 (C30) across 3168 (C29) reviews); success would show a drop in this issue's complaint count and share of complaints.
4. As a hedge, review premium-only restriction messaging and free-tier entitlement communication (severity sum 46246 (C06) across 15763 (C05) reviews), given billing/support's fastest-growing share of reviews (+10.2 pp (T12)); success would show this issue's share of reviews stop rising in the following year's comparison.

## Why

- Usability's severity sum 52983 (A05) is the highest among the four decision areas, narrowly ahead of billing/support's severity sum 52551 (A08), with playback/performance at 48060 (A11) and access/onboarding at 11300 (A17); its three largest issues (ads 23235 (C10), library/queue 16888 (C18), navigation 7795 (C30)) together account for most of that total.
- Usability's share of complaints 24.3% (T19) (interval 24.0% (T20) to 24.5% (T21)) does not overlap with billing/support's share of complaints 19.7% (T22) (interval 19.5% (T23) to 20.0% (T24)), so usability's share of complaints is higher within sampling error.
- Over the same months one year apart, usability's share of reviews fell from 11.1% (T16) to 10.0% (T17) (change -1.1 pp (T18)), while billing/support's share rose from 3.8% (T10) to 13.9% (T11) (change +10.2 pp (T12)), the largest increase among the four decision areas; this growth trend is a reason to monitor billing/support closely even though it does not overturn usability's higher baseline severity sum.
- The independent verifier, applied to a sample of 2000 (T41) reviews, orders decision areas differently: under verifier labels playback/performance leads on severity sum (758 (T46)), with usability second (747 (T47)) still ahead of billing/support (660 (T48)); under the enricher's own labels for that same sample, billing/support (705 (T42)) edges ahead of usability (650 (T43)). This is a labelling disagreement on a small sample, and it makes the recommendation provisional rather than a reason to depart from the full-dataset leader.
- Usability also shows a lift during the spike month 2023-10 (T35), where shuffle/repeat (usability.library) showed a lift of +1.4 pp (T40), alongside larger lifts for premium-only restrictions (+23.3 pp (T38)) and general complaints (+15.3 pp (T39)).

## What would change this call

- A larger verified sample confirming the verifier's severity-sum ordering (758 (T46), 747 (T47), 660 (T48)) rather than the current small-sample disagreement with enricher labels (705 (T42), 650 (T43)) would weaken confidence in usability's lead.
- If billing/support's share-of-reviews growth (from 3.8% (T10) to 13.9% (T11), change +10.2 pp (T12)) continues into the following year's same-months comparison and overtakes usability's share, that would favor billing/support instead.
- A human audit of the premium-only records without a payment word (upper-bound sensitivity on 2456 (T56), severity sum 7283 (T57)) finding most are genuinely about free-tier restrictions rather than mislabeled would strengthen billing/support's case relative to usability.

## Alternatives considered

- **billing/support**: Billing/support's severity sum 52551 (A08) is close to but below usability's 52983 (A05), and while its share of reviews grew the most among the four decision areas (from 3.8% (T10) to 13.9% (T11), change +10.2 pp (T12)) and its top issue is premium-only restrictions (severity sum 46246 (C06) across 15763 (C05) reviews), that issue is a free-tier packaging and entitlement policy question, not usability's core listening-experience defects, and the enricher-sample robustness check (705 (T42) vs 650 (T43)) is a small-sample difference that does not override the full-dataset severity-sum lead.
- **playback/performance**: Playback/performance's severity sum 48060 (A11) trails usability's 52983 (A05), and its share of reviews actually fell from 9.9% (T13) to 5.1% (T14) (change -4.8 pp (T15)) over the same months one year apart, even though the verifier's labels alone put it first on severity sum (758 (T46)).
- **access/onboarding**: Access/onboarding has by far the smallest severity sum (11300 (A17)) and share of complaints (3.2% (T28), interval 3.1% (T29) to 3.3% (T30)) among the four decision areas, despite a modest rise in share of reviews from 1.0% (T07) to 1.8% (T08) (change +0.8 pp (T09)).

## Limitations

- The verifier's disagreement on which area leads (playback/performance under its labels versus usability/billing under enricher labels) means the ranking among the top areas is not firmly settled and should be treated as provisional.
- The one-year share-of-reviews comparison is a descriptive pattern in self-selected reviews and cannot establish that usability issues are causing the underlying shift, only that billing/support's share grew fastest among the four decision areas while usability's declined.
- Spike-month lifts (for example +23.3 pp (T38), +15.3 pp (T39), +1.4 pp (T40)) describe single months against a median and do not by themselves indicate whether usability's issues are trending up or down outside those months.
- Unallocated complaints (computed by code from `area_rollup.json`): 29.7% (A19) of complaint and cancellation records (27137 (A01)) are general/unspecified: generic criticism (for example "worst update") that names no specific area, so they are not allocated to any of the four decision areas. If many of them refer to one area's problem, for example the same free-tier change, that area's total is understated; the labels cannot show which area that would be.
- Premium-only boundary, upper-bound sensitivity (computed by code; `analysis/premium_only_sensitivity.json`): 2456 (T56) of the 15763 (C05) `billing.premium_only` complaint and cancellation records contain no Premium or payment word (premium, subscri-, pay/paid/paying, free, buy, money, trial, $; case-insensitive), with a severity sum of 7283 (T57). If none of them belonged to billing/support, its severity sum would fall from 52551 (A08) to 45268 (T58), below usability 52983 (A05) and playback/performance 48060 (A11). This is an upper bound, not a relabelling: many of these reviews plainly describe free-tier limits without naming Premium (skip limits, forced shuffle), and they were not moved to any other area.
- Counts describe the labeled reviews in the declared analysis scope of 200,013 reviews (analysis_200k.csv, a deterministic uniform sample of the 660,622-review corpus plus every empty-text row): self-selected public app reviews, not a customer population. They carry no revenue, plan tier or confirmed cancellations, so revenue at risk cannot be computed.
- Cancellation language is expressed intent, not observed churn; nothing here estimates a causal effect on retention.
- Star ratings and helpful votes were not used to classify; severity is judged from the text under the shared rubric.
- 17828 completed records are flagged needs_review, 13 are quarantined and 0 are unfinished.
- The analysis covers a uniform random sample of the corpus, not every review; shares carry sampling error (intervals shown) and the 460,609 out-of-scope reviews were ingested but not classified.
