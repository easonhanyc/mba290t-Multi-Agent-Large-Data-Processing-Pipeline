You write a short decision memo for Spotify's product team, using only a saved aggregate table, descriptive trend figures and a small evidence pack.

The question: at the end of this historical review window, where should the next quarter of product-improvement effort go - access/onboarding, usability, playback/performance, or billing/support?

Rules:
- Use only the facts in the input JSON. Do not invent numbers, customers, revenue, churn, causes, trends, targets or dates.
- Never write a digit anywhere in your text. Refer to every number through its token exactly as given in the input, for example {C03}, {A02}, {S01} or {T04}. Dates are tokens too: write a spike month as its month token and the two comparison periods as their earlier_period and later_period tokens, or use plain words such as "the same months one year later" or "the following year". Never type a year, a month number, a quarter number (write "next quarter"), a confidence level or a severity level yourself. Code replaces each token with the saved value and appends the token ID in parentheses automatically, so write each token only in braces (never a bare token ID or value), keep tokens in running text rather than inside your own parentheses, and put words between neighbouring tokens. A token is always the exact string in a "token" field of the input: a letter and digits in braces, such as {T01}. Never put a key name or any other word in braces (write {T01}, not {earlier_period}).
- Refer to reviews only by the review IDs listed in the evidence pack, and only in cited_reviews.
- The baseline priority of an issue is the sum of severity over its complaint and cancellation reviews (count times mean severity). Areas are sums over their issues. Recommend the area the evidence supports. If you depart from the area with the highest baseline priority, say why using the trend or robustness figures on that same metric (severity sum); a different metric alone is not a reason to depart. The period comparison's share of reviews, the spike-month lifts, complaint counts and the size of a single issue count reviews or describe one issue, not an area's severity sum: they can support a recommendation but never by themselves justify departing from the area with the highest baseline priority. If you do depart, the first rationale point must name the area with the highest baseline priority and both severity sums, and give the severity-sum evidence for departing.
- Use trends_and_robustness, when present, to say whether the recommended area is growing or shrinking between the same months one year apart, whether its lead holds within the sampling intervals, and whether the independent verifier's labels give the same top area. The verifier robustness figures use the baseline metric (row-weighted severity sum); judge the top area on that metric, and treat complaint counts as secondary. A difference in a handful of sampled reviews is noise, not a reason to depart. Describe these as descriptive patterns in self-selected reviews, never as causes.
- Growth comparisons cover the four decision areas only. When you compare growth or change between periods, say "among the four decision areas" (for example "the largest increase among the four decision areas"); never write "any area", "all areas" or "of every area". Areas that are not decision options (period_comparison.non_decision_areas, such as general/unspecified) may be mentioned only as context and must be named as not being one of the four options.
- Sampling intervals: area_shares_with_sampling_intervals gives each decision area's share of complaints, its 95% interval, and interval_overlaps_with (computed by code). Never call two shares "close", "similar", "comparable" or "within sampling error" when their intervals do not overlap (the other area is not in interval_overlaps_with): say which share is higher. Use "within sampling error" only for areas whose intervals overlap.
- If the recommended area leads on severity sum but not on complaint count or share of complaints (or the reverse), say so plainly, naming which area leads on which measure.
- The verifier labels the same sampled reviews a second time, so a different top area under its labels is a labelling disagreement, not sampling noise: report it as a caveat that makes the recommendation provisional, never dismiss it as noise.
- Do not make relative comparisons (ratios, percentage growth, "doubled", "largest relative change") that the input does not give as a token; compare changes only as the percentage-point tokens given.
- Robustness comparisons stay within one labeler: compare enricher-label figures only with other enricher-label figures, and verifier-label figures only with other verifier-label figures (for example, the verifier's severity sum for one area against the verifier's severity sum for another). Never set an enricher-label value against a verifier-label value in one comparison, and never write "X versus Y" where X comes from one labeler and Y from the other.
- Share of complaints and share of reviews are different measures. A share_of_complaints token is a share of all complaint and cancellation reviews; a share_of_reviews token (the period comparison) and a spike month's pp_difference are shares of all classified reviews. Use the exact term of the token you cite and never call a share of reviews a "complaint share". For an area's share of complaints, cite the percent token in area_shares_with_sampling_intervals, not the decimal share_of_complaints in areas.
- Cite each token only for the quantity its key names. sample_reviews is the size of the verification sample, never a count of complaints; area tokens describe a whole area, never a single issue; an issue's C tokens describe that issue only. Where the input gives a fact without a token (for example top_by_complaint_count), state it in words and cite no token.
- Introduce every token with words that name what it measures (for example "severity sum {C02} across {C01} complaint and cancellation reviews"). Never join tokens with slashes, never list tokens without saying what each one measures, and never copy field names (such as same_top_area) into the prose.
- trends_and_robustness.premium_only_sensitivity, when present, is an upper-bound check computed by code: the premium-only complaint records whose text contains no Premium or payment word, their severity sum, and what billing/support's severity sum would be without them compared with the other decision areas. Cite its tokens in the robustness point of the rationale and in what_would_change_the_call (for example, a human audit of those reviews finding that many are not about free-tier restrictions would weaken billing/support). Those records are already counted in billing/support, so confirming them leaves its severity sum unchanged: never say an audit confirming them would strengthen or raise billing/support. Call it an upper bound, never a relabelling or a measured fact.
- Read each top issue through its definition and summary. In particular, the Premium-only restrictions issue (billing.premium_only) is customers objecting that features are locked to Premium (skip limits, forced shuffle, not being able to pick, repeat or rewind songs). It sits in billing/support in the taxonomy, but it is a free-tier packaging and entitlement policy question - what the free tier includes, and how changes to it are rolled out and explained - owned with pricing and monetization. It is not a payment, charging or checkout defect: never describe it as broken billing, failed payments or a support backlog.
- These are self-selected public app reviews. Cancellation language is expressed intent, not observed churn. Counts describe the labeled reviews in scope, not all customers.
- Code appends fixed limitations to the memo (self-selected reviews rather than a customer population, no revenue or plan data, expressed intent rather than churn, star ratings not used, needs_review and quarantine counts, sample scope, golden-set agreement, the share of complaints with no specific area in unallocated_complaints, and the premium-only sensitivity). Do not repeat them; your limitations should be the other ones specific to this decision.

Return:
- recommended_area: one of the four areas.
- recommendation: two or three sentences naming the area, its top issues with their tokens, and what kind of work it is (for billing.premium_only: free-tier packaging and entitlement policy, not a payment defect).
- next_quarter_actions: two to four concrete actions for the next quarter, each tied to a named top issue and its tokens, saying what to change or test and which saved measure (issue complaint count, severity sum, share of complaints or trend share) should move if it works. No invented targets, owners' names or dates. At most one action may hedge the strongest alternative area.
- rationale: three to five short points (size and severity of the top issues, trend, robustness).
- what_would_change_the_call: one to three specific pieces of evidence that would change or weaken the recommendation, citing the tokens they refer to - for example the verifier's severity ordering holding on a larger verified sample, a human audit of the premium-only records without a payment word (premium_only_sensitivity), the recommended area's trend share reversing in the same months of the next year, or the spike month's lift fading back to the other months' level.
- alternatives: one entry for each other area, with why it is not first, citing its tokens.
- limitations: two to four short points about what this evidence cannot show for this decision, other than the ones code appends (in particular not the premium-only sensitivity or the unallocated complaints).
- cited_reviews: review IDs from the evidence pack that support the recommendation.

Return only the JSON object required by the schema.
